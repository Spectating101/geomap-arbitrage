# Open-source economy thesis

GeoMap's strongest thesis is not “find secret arbitrage.” It is almost the opposite:

> Economic opportunity discovery can itself be open infrastructure.

A useful local economy contains thousands of mismatches that are difficult for an individual to see: businesses with small unsolved problems, skills that are locally scarce, assets that sit idle, products priced differently across channels, demand that occurs at inconvenient times, remote buyers with higher willingness to pay, and information that is public but fragmented.

Most people cannot continuously search all of those surfaces. They rely on generic advice, personal networks, or luck.

GeoMap attempts to reduce that search cost by representing opportunity as structured, falsifiable objects:

```text
archetype
+ local evidence
+ person constraints
+ full-cost economics
+ legal/execution constraints
→ testable opportunity thesis
```

## What “open source” means here

The commons is not a guaranteed-profit list. It is the reusable knowledge needed to investigate and execute an opportunity responsibly:

- what kind of mismatch might exist;
- what evidence would demonstrate it;
- what person/resources it fits;
- which costs are easy to forget;
- which rules or permits matter;
- how to test it cheaply;
- what failure looks like;
- what evidence would invalidate the thesis.

A community could therefore improve the *method and encyclopedia* even when local market instances differ.

## Why this can still create private economic gain

Open knowledge does not eliminate execution differences. Two people may see the same opportunity but differ in:

- location;
- skill;
- language;
- trust/network;
- speed;
- equipment;
- capital;
- available time;
- willingness to do unattractive operational work;
- ability to automate or standardize delivery.

The system therefore maps opportunities to profiles rather than pretending one opportunity is universally valuable.

## Anti-thesis

GeoMap should fail if it degenerates into any of the following:

- scraped “side hustle” listicles;
- fake precision about expected income;
- recommendations based on one unverified source;
- gross price spread that ignores real costs;
- hidden regulatory assumptions;
- a map of businesses to spam;
- financial-market trade signals disguised as livelihood advice;
- an LLM inventing opportunities without inspectable evidence.

The project is successful when it becomes an evidence-backed **economic search and execution layer**, not when it produces the largest list of ideas.
